# 007 Flashlight: research

Question: why does a flashlight turn on when you click it?

Demand: board keyword not listed yet ("flashlight" has no chart); backlog pick (top item "phone battery in the cold" kept for its Sat Nov 7 / Nov 9 to 14 window). Signal: NO DATA. Category: Home / Gear. Related episode: 002-clicky-pen (same latch idea).

## Claims and sources
| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| Electricity flows from the battery's plus end through the lamp and back to the minus end (a full loop) | Panther Vision, "How Does a Flashlight Work?": "allowing electricity to flow from the positive terminal of the battery throughout the lamp and back to the negative terminal of the battery." https://panthervision.com/blogs/news/how-does-a-flashlight-work |
| Cells in series (nose to tail) | NZCER ARBS "How a torch works" marking key: "2 cells", "A series circuit is drawn." https://arbs.nzcer.org.nz/resources/how-torch-works |
| Tail spring keeps the batteries pressed in place / good contact, and is part of the circuit | ARBS: "Spring: to make sure there are good contacts/keep batteries connected or firmly in place." Olight: "the negative terminal (typically a metal spring in the tail cap)." |
| A metal strip runs along the flashlight and connects batteries, lamp and switch | Panther Vision: "a thin strip of copper or brass metal that creates an electrical connection between the batteries, lamp and switch", "contact strips that run up and down the length of the flashlight." ARBS: "Metal strip: to complete the circuit." |
| Off = a gap (strips separated); click = metal contact bridges it | Panther Vision: switch "makes contact between two of the contact strips"; off, "the contact strips are physically separated, breaking the pathway." CPF forward clickie teardown: contacts "closed by the spring supported metal disk." |
| A clicky tail switch has the same kind of parts as a clicky pen | Candlepower Forums, "Anatomy of a forward clickie": "it works similar to the inside of a ball point pen", "The clicky parts look a lot like a ball point pen inside." https://www.candlepowerforums.com/threads/anatomy-of-a-forward-clickie-photos-more-added-1-29-08.187895/ |
| Flicker when shaken: a thin layer of oxidation (rust) on the contacts; fix with a pencil eraser | Olight, "How to clean flashlight battery corrosion": "If your flashlight flickers when moved ... the electrical path is likely obstructed by a thin layer of oxidation", "use a pencil eraser to rub the contact point." https://www.olight.com/blog/how-to-clean-flashlight-battery-corrosion-rusty-contacts ; Hunker: "Running a pencil eraser over the contact plate will remove rust and grime." https://www.hunker.com/12480355/how-to-fix-an-led-flashlight |

Simplification: some flashlights use the metal body as the return path instead of a strip; the script uses the strip (both cited sources) and says nothing about the body.
