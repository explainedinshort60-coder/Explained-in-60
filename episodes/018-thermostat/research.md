## Sources
Note: energy.gov and Wikipedia were not fetchable this run (fetch permission prompt with nobody to answer); HowStuffWorks returned an empty page. The script uses only claims the two readable sources support. No claims about mercury switches, 24 V wiring, digital sensors or anticipators are made.

- A Moment of Science (Indiana University / Indiana Public Media), "How does a thermostat work?", Eric Sonstroem, 2019-09-13: https://www.ipm.org/show/amomentofscience/2019-09-13/thermostat-work
  - The coil is "two long, thin strips of metal, usually brass and steel, that have been joined firmly together".
  - "the brass expands and contracts a little bit more than the steel does over the same range of temperature"
  - "if the coil tightens as it cools, the free end can curl toward a metal contact that starts the furnace."
  - "As the air heats up and the coil loosens, the free end curls away from the contact and the furnace shuts off."
  - Moving the contact changes the temperature needed to trigger the furnace (that is the setting).
- Lennox, "Thermostat location" (Jun 26, 2026): https://www.lennox.com/residential/lennox-life/consumer/thermostat-location
  - "It senses the air around it and tells your HVAC system what to do based on that reading."
  - "Sunlight streaming through a window can raise the surface temperature around your thermostat"
  - "A thermostat mounted near a floor lamp or behind a television will pick up that warmth"
  - "a thermostat near a drafty exterior door will sense cold air and signal your furnace to run"
  - Best spot: "an interior wall, roughly 4 to 5 feet off the floor".

## Script claims vs sources
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Coil of brass and steel stuck together | IPM |
| Brass shrinks more in the cold | IPM (brass contracts more than steel) |
| Room cools, coil curls tighter, tip touches a metal contact | IPM |
| The contact starts the furnace ("closes a circuit" is the plain meaning of two metal parts touching to start it) | IPM |
| Room warms, coil loosens, tip pulls away, furnace shuts off | IPM |
| Turning the dial slides the contact closer or farther | IPM (moving the contact sets the temperature) |
| It only feels the air next to it; sun or a lamp make it think it's warm; a draft makes the heat run | Lennox |

## Reference shapes (what is drawn)
- Classic round wall thermostat: round ivory body, a dial ring with a set pointer, behind a clear cover a flat spiral coil fixed at the center, its free outer end carrying a small arm, and a contact post on a ring the dial turns.
- Bimetal strip close-up: two bonded layers; cooling bends it toward the brass side (brass on the inside of the curve), because brass shrinks more.
- Furnace: a box on the floor with a flame window and a warm-air vent; one wire runs from the thermostat to it.
