## Sources (mechanism)
- Physics Van, University of Illinois, "Why do Basketballs Bounce?": https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/103
  - Balls "bounce because of the pressurized air inside of them."
  - "The extra air pressure pushes against the bottom of the ball, making it push harder against the ground."
  - Without that air pressure, "most of the energy just goes into bending the molecules in the rubber of the ball", which "just heats the ball up."
- Allen and Allen, "Modeling the bounce of a gas-filled ball" (arXiv 2202.03034): https://arxiv.org/html/2202.03034v2
  - "the deformed ball behaves like a compressed spring."
  - Energy is lost through "conversion to thermal energy and sound".
- University of Alaska Fairbanks physics course page, "Bouncing balls (dribbling)": https://ffden-2.phys.uaf.edu/211_fall2004.web.dir/Drew_Olson_Dean_Towarak/dribbling.htm
  - "the force of the floor hitting back on the basketball causes the basketball to indent", then "the ball [inflates] back to its original volume and rebound[s] off of the floor into the air."
  - "some of the kinetic energy is lost and is transferred into thermal energy."

## Sources (cold ball bonus)
- Physics Van, "Temperature Affecting Bouncy Balls": https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/94
  - "A hot ball will usually have more air pressure in it than the same ball, colder (unless it's leaky), because air likes to expand when hot."
- Physics Van, "Ideal Gas Law in Basketballs": https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/18696
  - With the same air inside, "when the temperature drops the product of Pressure and Volume has to drop."
- Socratic, "Why does a basketball bounce differently inside a gym than it does outside on a cold day?": https://socratic.com/questions/why-does-a-basketball-bounce-differently-inside-a-gym-than-it-does-outside-on-a-
  - "On a cold day, the pressure inside the basketball decreases" and it "does not bounce as high as it does in a warm room."

## Sources (pebbles)
- US Patent 11,097,164, "Basketball having improved pebbled texture" (Wilson Sporting Goods, 2021): https://patents.justia.com/patent/11097164
  - A basketball "typically includes a pebbled texture to improve the gripability of the ball"; grip affects "the player's ability to catch, retain or dribble the ball".

## Reference shapes (ATM)
- Basketball: sphere with 8 panels; seen side on, one horizontal seam, one vertical seam, two curved side seams (C shapes). Surface covered in small round pebbles. Seam channels are dark.
- Bounce: on contact the bottom flattens into a flat circle and the ball widens; the flat spot is largest at the lowest point, then the ball reforms and leaves the floor.
- Flat ball: sags on the floor like a pancake, wrinkled skin, dull thud, almost no rebound.
