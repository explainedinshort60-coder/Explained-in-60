# Fact sheet: 024 jar lid (You've Been Doing It Wrong)
Topic source: Week 2 series queue, Tue Nov 10 slot A (plans/week2.md, approved 2026-11-06). Demand: US Google Trends Nov index 1.84 for the topic (plans/week2.md); holiday cooking season (pickles, salsa, cranberry sauce, gravy jars).

| Claim in script | Source |
|---|---|
| A sealed jar holds a vacuum: less air (lower pressure) inside than outside | Google Patents US7328731B2 "Vacuum release device and method" https://patents.google.com/patent/US7328731 : the jar interior "is much lower than atmospheric pressure"; "the lid may be at least partially held against the containment volume by the reduced pressure of the vacuum seal itself" |
| The outside air presses the lid down onto the glass; the seal is jammed tight and twisting fights friction | US7328731B2: "a large force pressing the sealing surface to the lip of the jar due to a differential pressure"; "vacuum-induced friction forces at the sealing surface" |
| Let air in and the seal breaks (the hiss), which removes the push | US7328731B2: letting air in equalizes pressure, "substantially eliminating the normal force on the jar lid closure"; "removal of the lid may involve both releasing the vacuum seal and unscrewing the lid" |
| Hot water on the metal lid: metal grows more than glass when heated, the lid loosens a little | HyperPhysics (Georgia State Univ.) "Thermal Expansion: How to Loosen a Jar Lid" https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/thermo/jarlid.html : "Directing the stream of hot water at the center of the lid heats the metal more than the glass", "the thermal expansion coefficient of the metal is greater than that of the glass"; ScienceABC https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/how-does-hot-water-help-to-remove-a-metal-lid-from-a-glass-jar.html : "metal stretches more than glass for every degree of warming" (aluminum about 23 x 10^-6 per °C, glass about 9 x 10^-6) |
| "Just a hair" | HyperPhysics worked example: the gain on a 7 cm lid is tiny (fractions of a millimeter), so the VO says "just a hair" and claims no number |

Myth (Blue): "Just squeeze harder." Not claimed as harmful in the VO; the VO only says the air, not grip, is the problem.
Safety: a spoon, never a knife (plan rule); hot TAP water only, no boiling water, no banging the glass. Flat diagram: jar cross-section with air arrows; no injury shown.
Not used (unverified this run): pounds-of-force numbers; how canning forms the vacuum (NCHFP page blocked by fetch permission); "tap the lid" and rubber-grip tips.
Reference images (ATM): grocery-store glass jar with a 4-lug tinplate twist cap (pickle or salsa jar), lid underside with the sealing compound ring, cross-section diagram of a lug cap on the jar finish (thread lugs, seal ring on the glass lip), a teaspoon edge slid under the lid skirt.
