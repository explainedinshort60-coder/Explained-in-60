# 009 Windshield frost: research

Question: why is there frost on your windshield when the air is above freezing?

Demand: RUSH from the trend scout 2026-11-02 (score 8). Trend: first freeze week, cold air across the northern and eastern US (Fox Weather "Cold air invades US to start November", AccuWeather cold wave). Chart: "frost on windshield" reads 0 vs the microwave anchor (below resolution), supply 40; the signal is the weather news, not the chart. Season: rises with every clear, calm fall and winter night until spring. Category: Car. Related episode: 008-cold-battery.

## Claims and sources
| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| The air reading is taken about four feet up; the ground-level surface can be colder | WMC Action News 5, "Breakdown: Why temperature is key in frost formation" (2019-12-11): "the air temperatures are usually taken at about four feet above the ground, where it can be warmer than temperatures at ground level." https://www.actionnews5.com/2019/12/11/breakdown-why-temperature-is-key-frost-formation/ |
| The surface loses heat by (infrared, invisible) radiation, fastest under a clear sky | Action News 5: frost forms "when a surface cools through a loss of infrared radiation to a temperature which is colder than the dew point ... and the temperature of that surface is below freezing." WATE 6 "How does frost form? What is radiational cooling?" (2021-12-01): "This process happens faster under clear skies." https://digital-release.wate.com/weather-news/weather-school/how-does-frost-form-what-is-radiational-cooling/amp/ ; Naked Scientists: "at night - especially if there's a clear sky at night - anything which is warm radiates heat." |
| The glass can be below freezing while the air is above it (37 F air) | WATE 6: "For frost to form the surface temperature must be between 30° – 32°F", "the air temperature can be a little bit warmer around 35° to 37°F. This is because the surface cools first and faster than the air above it." |
| Water vapor turns straight into ice on the glass | Action News 5: frost is water vapor "deposited onto a surface as ice"; "The moisture comes from water vapor in the air." |
| A carport roof is warmer than the sky and radiates heat back down onto the car | The Naked Scientists, "Why would an open carport stop frost?": the roof is "still a lot, lot warmer than the sky" and "is still radiating some heat down onto the ground, inside the carport." https://www.thenakedscientists.com/articles/questions/why-would-open-carport-stop-frost |
| Clouds act like a blanket | WATE 6: "clouds provide a blanket keeping the heat on the ground." |
| A tree does the same | Action News 5: "trees put out more radiation toward the ground than does the clear sky." |

Simplifications: "a clear sky sends back barely any" heat (sources: clear sky speeds the loss; trees and clouds send more back). The windshield is said to "lie back" and face the sky (it is sloped; Chad Orzel's post notes the gently sloped windshield frosts more than the side windows, a blog, used only for the staging, not as a spoken claim). Calm wind is also needed (Action News 5), not mentioned in the script. No "it is always X degrees" claim; the dial shows 30 F, inside WATE's 30 to 32 F surface range.
