## Sources (mechanism)
- AP via Spectrum News 1, "College football helmet communication", Aug 26, 2024: https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/news/2024/08/26/college-football-helmet-communication
  - "Coaches can relay plays to the helmet of a designated player on either side of the ball up to 15 seconds before the play clock expires."
  - "communication will be turned off with 15 seconds remaining on the play clock or when the ball is snapped, whichever comes first."
  - "A single player on offense and a single player on defense will be able to hear from a coach." "Designated players will sport a green dot on their helmets."
  - The NFL "implemented transmitters between coaches and QBs in 1994 and then defensive players 14 years later" (= 2008).
- Fourverts Football, "Do NFL players have speakers in their helmets?": https://fourvertsfootball.com/do-nfl-players-have-speakers-in-their-helmets/
  - "The speakers in NFL players' helmets do not allow them to talk with the coaching staff."
  - Quoting the rulebook: communication "is cut off when the play clock reaches 15 seconds or the ball is snapped, whichever occurs first."
  - "On offense this player is going to be the quarterback and on defense this player is going to be the middle linebacker." Players with the speaker have "a small green sticker applied to their helmets."
- SYNCO, "How do coaches communicate with players (NFL)", Apr 4, 2026: https://www.syncoaudio.com/blogs/news/how-do-coaches-communicate-with-players-nfl
  - Coaches "speak into the football coach headset"; the helmet receiver has "built-in speakers".
  - "The system is called 'one-way' because players can only listen to the coach and cannot talk back."
  - "The radio connection starts at the beginning of the 40-second play clock and is automatically cut off when there are 15 seconds left." Radios "introduced in 1994".
- Battle Sports, "What does a green dot on a football helmet mean", Apr 6, 2026: https://battlesports.com/blogs/news/what-does-a-green-dot-on-a-football-helmet-mean
  - "Coaches deliver instruction through their headsets, and the player listens through a tiny speaker near their ear."
  - The dot "signals that a radio receiver is built inside the helmet." "each team may have only one radio-equipped player on the field for its offensive or defensive unit."
