# Research: zipper (staggered teeth, Y-channel slider, bump and dent)

## Sources (mechanism)
- Wikipedia, "Zipper": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipper
  - Slider "contains a Y-shaped channel that, by moving along the rows of teeth, meshes or separates them, depending on the direction of the slider's movement." It "acts on the teeth one-by-one".
  - Teeth: "a material part ("external projection") and a space ("internal recess")"; snug fit when "one member nests within the recess of an adjoining member".
  - Sundback 1917 patent (quoted): "the members on one stringer alternate with those on the other".
  - The snug fit "is stable ... also to transverse and longitudinal (both perpendicular) forces"; teeth are sized so "the forces which act on the zipper when the garment it is sewn on is worn cannot unlock the teeth."
  - Failure: "Most often, the zipper fails to close due to a worn or bent slider not being able to apply the necessary force to the sides of the teeth to cause them to interlock." Fix: "small pliers to carefully squeeze the back part of the slider together a fraction of a millimeter" (warns the slider can break if squeezed hard).
  - Top stop: "two devices affixed to the top end of a zipper, to prevent the slider from coming off the chain". Bottom stop at the base.
  - Name: "zipper ... came from the B. F. Goodrich Company in 1923".
- CEO Zipper, "The Anatomy of a Zipper": https://ceozipper.com/blog/parts-of-a-zipper/
  - "Inside its body, a Y-shaped channel forces the teeth together on the way up and apart on the way down."
  - Top stops are "small clamps at the top end of the teeth on each tape"; bottom stop "joins the two tapes permanently at the base".
- National Inventors Hall of Fame, Gideon Sundback: https://invent.org/inductees/gideon-sundback
  - "He faced two rows of teeth opposite each other and added a slider to pull them together." Teeth made by cutting "scoops" and "punching dimples". Goodrich coined "zipper" in 1923.
- Smithsonian NMAH, Sundback patent model (US 1,219,881, 1917): https://www.si.edu/object/nmah_860486 (interlocking metal teeth; Goodrich credited with the name).
- Low-reliability, not used for claims: NCF India blog (wedge wording): https://blog.ncf-india.org/saw/zippers-how-do-they-work

## Disagreements and choices
- "Wedge" or "diamond" inside the slider appears only in the low-reliability blog. The script says "Y-shaped tunnel" (Wikipedia, CEO Zipper). The drawing shows the centre post of the Y as a small dark triangle but it is never named.
- "Bump on top, dent underneath": Wikipedia says projection and recess; invent.org says scoops with dimples. Script uses the plain words "bump" and "dent".
- "You can't rip it apart sideways" is phrased as a demo ("Nope", "does nothing") rather than an absolute strength claim; Wikipedia supports stability against transverse forces in normal wear.
- Pliers fix: script says "pinch it a hair tighter", matching "a fraction of a millimeter". No claim about which end beyond the drawing.

## Reference images and shape facts

Shape facts come from the text above and common product photos described in the CEO Zipper anatomy list: tape, teeth (chain), slider body with pull tab, top stops, bottom stop.
- Drawing rules used: two ivory tapes with teeth alternating half a pitch apart; closed teeth overlap across the centre line; above the slider the rows open in a V; slider wide at the top (two arms of the Y) and narrow at the bottom (joined stem); pull tab hangs from a bridge on the slider face; top stops as two small clamps, bottom stop as one bar joining both tapes.
