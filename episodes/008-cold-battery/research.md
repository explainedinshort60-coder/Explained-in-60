## Sources (mechanism)
- The Conversation via CBS News, Wesley Chang (Drexel University), "Lithium-ion batteries and cold weather, expert explains", Mar 5, 2024: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/lithium-ion-batteries-cold-weather-expert-explains
  - "Batteries contain fluids called electrolytes, and cold temperatures cause fluids to flow more slowly."
  - "So, the electrolytes in batteries slow and thicken in the cold, causing the lithium ions inside to move slower."
  - "This slowdown can prevent the lithium ions from properly inserting into the electrodes."
- Fortune, Robert Hackett, "Why your phone battery dies in the cold", Feb 1, 2019: https://fortune.com/2019/02/01/phone-battery-cold-dies-why
  - "Cooling causes the electrolytic medium to thicken and become more viscous" (Linda Nazar, University of Waterloo), "reducing the mobility of the charged particles that drive the battery's chemical engine."
  - "the charge could drop precipitously, without warning, from 30% to...well, zero."
  - Quoting Apple: "this condition is temporary." "Once the battery's temperature returns to its normal operating range, its performance will return to normal as well".
- Farmer's Almanac, Maggie Konrad, "Why Your Phone Battery Suddenly Dies Faster in Cold Weather", updated Oct 10, 2026: https://www.farmersalmanac.com/phone-battery-cold-weather
  - "The battery has a harder time delivering the electrical power your phone needs"; "Cold can temporarily reduce how much power the battery can deliver."
  - "Sometimes, it even shuts off before the battery reaches zero." "the phone shutting down even though the screen previously showed some charge remaining." "That doesn't necessarily mean the battery has permanently lost all that energy."
  - "Normal battery performance generally returns when the device reaches a warmer temperature."
  - "carry it in an inside coat pocket rather than an exposed outer pocket"; "Keeping it closer to your body can help protect it from freezing air."
  - "Don't try to speed things up with a hair dryer, heating pad, or another direct heat source."

## Disagreements and choices
- Operating ranges differ by source and date (Almanac: 32 to 95 F for Apple and Samsung; Fortune quoting an older Apple page: 62 to 72 F comfort zone). Left out of the script.
- Internal resistance and voltage sag are the textbook mechanism but were not verified this run, so the script says only "can't keep up" (power delivery), which the sources support.
