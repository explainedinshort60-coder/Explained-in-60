# Fact sheet: 023 wash turkey (You've Been Doing It Wrong)
Topic source: Week 2 series queue, Mon Nov 9 slot B (plans/week2.md ep 13, approved 2026-11-06). Demand: US Google Trends Nov index for "washing turkey" 4.90 (Nov interest / yearly average, 2021 to 2025, plans/week2.md); Thanksgiving Thu Nov 26, so the search collapses after Nov 27.

| Claim in script | Source |
|---|---|
| Rinsing raw poultry does not remove or destroy the bacteria | USDA blog, "Don't Wash Poultry, But If You Do..." (2019-08-20) https://www.usda.gov/node/30867 : "Washing, rinsing, or brining meat and poultry in salt water, vinegar or lemon juice does not destroy bacteria." |
| Rinsing splashes bacteria around the kitchen (sink, counter, nearby food) | CDC via Fox News (2023-11-22) https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/cdc-cooking-thanksgiving-turkey-food-safety : "Raw turkey should never be washed or rinsed as it presents a risk for bacteria to splash around your kitchen"; USDA blog (salad contamination below) |
| A USDA study watched people rinse raw poultry; 60% had bacteria in the sink after | USDA blog: "Of the participants who washed their raw poultry, 60% had bacteria in their sink after washing or rinsing the poultry." |
| 26% got it onto their salad | USDA blog: "26% of salads were then contaminated by those participants that washed the chicken." |
| Only heat kills them: cook to 165°F, check with a thermometer | USDA blog: "Destroy any illness causing bacteria by cooking meat and poultry to a safe internal temperature as measured by a food thermometer" and "Poultry (whole or ground) are safe to eat at 165°F."; CDC via Fox News: thermometer to a safe internal temperature of 165°F |
| Scrub the sink with hot, soapy water | USDA blog: "Clean sinks and countertops with hot soapy water and then apply a sanitizer." |

Context (not in the VO): CDC (via Fox News) cites a 2020 survey in which 78% of participants reported washing or rinsing turkey before cooking, which is why this myth is the stake. The same USDA study found 31% of non-washers still got bacteria onto their salad (hand and surface contamination), so the VO only claims the washers' number and stays exact.
Not used (unverified this run): "up to 3 feet" splash distance; pat-dry advice; thermometer placement. FSIS, CDC and MSU pages could not be fetched (permission timeout or bot challenge).

Safety: no illness shown, germs are mild cartoon blobs, no gross detail; a flat kitchen diagram only.
